Air source heat pumps

Assessment, design and installation — plus a straight answer on whether your property is ready for one.

A straight answer first

I work on gas and on heat pumps, which means I've no reason to talk you into either. Plenty of Scottish properties are a good fit for a heat pump. Plenty of others need work on insulation and radiators before one makes sense, and the fastest way to end up with an expensive system that disappoints you is to skip that conversation.

So the first step is an assessment, not a quote.

What the assessment covers

  • Room-by-room heat loss. The whole design follows from this. Sizing from floor area or from the old boiler's output is how oversized, short-cycling systems happen.
  • Emitter sizing. Each radiator checked against its room's heat loss at the design flow temperature — typically 45–50 °C rather than a boiler's 70 °C.
  • Hot water strategy. Heat pumps and combis don't mix; you need a cylinder, with a coil sized for a heat pump rather than a boiler.
  • Siting and noise. Where the unit goes, clearance for airflow, condensate drainage, and distance to boundaries and windows.
  • Electrical capacity. Supply, consumer unit and whether the network operator needs notifying.

Design and installation

System design, emitter upgrades, cylinder, pipework and commissioning. The aim is a system that runs at the lowest flow temperature the house allows, because that's what determines the efficiency you'll actually get — not the number on the brochure.

Working alongside gas

Not every job is all-or-nothing. Where a full changeover isn't right yet, I'll tell you what the sensible order of works looks like — usually fabric and emitters first — so you're not paying twice.

Flow temperature matters

Flow tempEffect
35 °CBest efficiency — needs underfloor or large emitters
45 °CRealistic retrofit target with upsized radiators
55 °CWorks, but efficiency and running cost both suffer

Every 1 °C you can take off the flow temperature is efficiency you keep for the life of the system.

Heat pump questions

Will a heat pump work in an old Scottish house?

Sometimes, and sometimes not yet. A heat pump runs at a much lower flow temperature than a gas boiler, so it needs bigger emitters and a reasonably insulated fabric to keep up. In a solid-wall tenement with small single panel radiators, the honest answer is often to fix the fabric and the emitters first.

Are they cheaper to run than gas?

It depends entirely on the seasonal efficiency you achieve and the gap between electricity and gas unit rates. A well-designed system running at a low flow temperature can beat gas. A badly designed one running flat out at 55 °C will cost you more. The design is the whole game.

Do I need to replace all my radiators?

Not always all of them, but usually some. Each room gets a heat loss calculation and the emitter is sized to deliver that at the design flow temperature. Rooms that were marginal on a boiler will need upsizing.

Is there grant funding available?

Scotland has its own funding routes, which are separate from the English schemes and change periodically. Check the current Home Energy Scotland offering before committing — and note that grant-funded installations generally require an MCS certified installer.

How noisy are they?

A modern unit is roughly comparable to a fridge at a few metres. Siting matters more than the headline figure — boundary distances and where your neighbour's bedroom window is are the practical constraints.
