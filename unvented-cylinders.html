Unvented hot water cylinders

Mains-pressure hot water — supplied, installed, commissioned and serviced by a G3 registered engineer.

Proper pressure at the tap

An unvented cylinder does away with the loft tank and feeds hot water straight off the mains. The result is a shower that behaves like a shower, hot and cold at matched pressure, and no more waiting on gravity to do the work.

It's the right answer for a lot of houses. It isn't the right answer for all of them, and the deciding factor is almost always the incoming mains.

What I check before quoting

  • Mains flow rate. Measured, not guessed. Below 20 l/min an unvented cylinder will underperform no matter what you spend on it.
  • Cupboard dimensions. Diameter, clear height and door opening. A 600 mm deep cupboard forces a slimline cylinder, and some models need an external expansion vessel roughly 355 mm across that has to go somewhere.
  • Discharge route. The safety discharge has to go somewhere legal. Where an external route isn't practical, a correctly sized dry-trap tundish lets it discharge to an internal waste instead.
  • Electrical supply. Direct cylinders run two immersion heaters, typically one on an off-peak circuit. Both need to be live and serviceable before the old cylinder comes out.

Installation and sign-off

Every installation is commissioned, tested and certified, and you get the paperwork and the benchmark record for the manufacturer's warranty. Discharge pipework is installed in the correct materials — the primary discharge leg is metal, not plastic, and the receiving waste is solvent weld rather than push-fit.

Servicing and repairs

Annual servicing on any make of unvented cylinder: expansion vessel charge checked and recharged, pressure reducing valve and expansion relief tested, temperature and pressure relief valve exercised, immersions and thermostats checked, and the whole lot recorded on the benchmark.

Typical faults I get called to: dripping tundish, poor flow at the taps, water hammer, no hot water on one immersion, and pressure relief discharging when it shouldn't.

G3 registered

Unvented hot water storage is notifiable work requiring a G3 qualified engineer. I hold it — so the installation is commissioned, tested and certified properly.

Cylinder questions

What is an unvented cylinder?

A sealed hot water cylinder fed straight off the incoming mains, so your hot taps and shower run at mains pressure. No cold tank in the loft, no gravity, and balanced pressure between hot and cold.

Why does it need a special qualification?

Because it stores a large volume of water above 60 °C under pressure. If the controls fail and nothing stops it, the consequences are severe. The G3 qualification exists for that reason and it's a legal requirement to install or work on one.

What mains flow rate do I need?

As a working minimum, 20 litres per minute. Below that the cylinder will refill too slowly to keep up with a decent shower and you'll be disappointed regardless of what you spend. I measure it before recommending anything.

Will it fit in my airing cupboard?

Often, but it's worth measuring first. A standard 150 litre cylinder is around 550 mm diameter; slimline versions come down to about 475–490 mm. A 600 mm deep cupboard forces a slimline, and you want roughly 1800 mm of clear height to get the safety group in above it.

Do unvented cylinders need servicing?

Yes, annually. The expansion vessel loses charge, the pressure reducing valve drifts and the temperature and pressure relief valve needs exercising. An unserviced cylinder is where the failures start.
